## Analysis

The QQQ "geopolitical fear fade" pattern relies on the large-cap tech sector's balance sheet resilience. When price action is driven by macro headlines rather than tech fundamentals, a mean reversion recovery is a high-probability trade.

However, recent evidence from March 2026 has solidified two non-negotiable boundaries:
1. **The 200 SMA is the Definitive Floor:** Mean reversion call theses are invalidated instantly if the 200-day SMA breaks. Temporary geopolitical "dips" become structural regime shifts once this level is lost. Reclaiming the level (as seen on March 23) offers a high-conviction re-entry, but failing to hold it (as seen on March 24) is a final invalidation of the recovery thesis for that cycle.
2. **Trend Persistence & External Drivers:** A technical breakdown (e.g., price below 50 SMA) remains active even if the news catalyst (e.g., oil price spike, geopolitical pause) fades. Structural trend and macro data (e.g., morning economic release) have higher priority than news status.

The failed mean-reversion re-entries on March 23/24 ($595C, $600C) confirm that while "geopolitical relief" (the 5-day Iran pause) can trigger a reclamation of the 200 SMA, it is fragile. The rejection of this level on negative morning economic data on March 24 demonstrates that **macro technical floors override news catalysts**. Immediate exit upon the break of the reclaimed 200 SMA is a mandatory capital preservation rule.
