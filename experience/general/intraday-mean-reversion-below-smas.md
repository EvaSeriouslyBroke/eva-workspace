# Intraday Mean Reversion Below Major SMAs

**Thesis:** Intraday "relief rallies" or "oversold bounces" in a structural bear regime (price below 50 and 200-day SMAs) are high-probability bull traps; technical gravity from overhead resistance (SMAs) usually overrides short-term news catalysts, making quick exits at resistance mandatory.

**Applies to:** general, QQQ, IWM
**Tags:** mean-reversion, bull-trap, below-sma, bear, dte-short
**Confidence:** medium
**Last Updated:** 2026-04-01

## Analysis

In a structural bear market (defined by price trading below both the 50-day and 200-day Simple Moving Averages), the market's "Technical Gravity" is heavily weighted to the downside. While geopolitical de-escalation or positive news headlines can trigger sharp "relief rallies" and RSI recoveries from oversold levels (<30), these moves frequently fail as they approach the 200-day SMA from below.

The 200-day SMA acts as a definitive "ceiling" in this regime. Any intraday long (calls) entered on a tactical reversal thesis must be treated as a "scalp" with a hard exit target at or just below the 200-day SMA. Failure to reclaim and hold this level on high volume confirms the continuation of the bearish regime, regardless of the news sentiment.

## Exceptions & Nuances

- **High-Volume Reclamation:** If a stock reclaims the 200-day SMA on significantly higher-than-average volume and holds it for more than 4 hours of trading, the bear regime may be transitioning.
- **broader Market Alignment:** If SPY and QQQ both reclaim their respective 200-day SMAs simultaneously, the probability of a sustained reversal increases.
